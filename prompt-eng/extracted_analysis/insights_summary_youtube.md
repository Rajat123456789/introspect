• Viewing Patterns  
  – “Addiction” and “Rabbit Holes” dominate overall watch counts (40–60% of videos), peaking on Mondays and Saturdays—risking start‑of‑week and weekend overuse.  
  – “Escapism” peaks midweek (Wednesdays) and early week (Mondays), suggesting response to work stress or “Monday blues.”  
  – “Doom scrolling” is less frequent but spikes mid‑week and pre‑weekend, correlating modestly with higher addiction scores.  
  – Late‑night viewing (10 PM–4 AM) has risen steadily, especially around 2–3 AM—likely disrupting sleep and exacerbating low morning mood.  

• Content Preferences & Sentiment  
  – Highest positive impact: Self‑improvement/personal growth and motivation/mental strength videos (avg. sentiment ≈ 0.8–0.9).  
  – Moderate benefit: Mindfulness, mental health experiences, and therapy/support content (avg. 0.65–0.75).  
  – Lowest uplift: Anxiety, depression, and stress‑management topics (avg. sentiment ≈ 0.45–0.55)—may reflect inherent distress in these subjects.  
  – Engaging “informative” content tends to be less detrimental than “trending,” “viral,” or “controversial” content, which often align with lower wellbeing scores.  

• Temporal & Emotional Triggers  
  – Mornings (0 AM–8 AM) consistently show the lowest mental‑health scores; evenings (5 PM–8 PM) show the highest—suggesting diurnal mood swings.  
  – Weeks with intense addiction peaks (e.g., weeks 2, 38, 51) and sustained heatmap “hot” days flag periods for proactive check‑ins.  
  – Early 2025 saw pronounced surges in addiction and escapism metrics—indicative of stress or burnout phases.  

• Actionable Recommendations  
  – Enforce a wind‑down routine to curb late‑night viewing (e.g., screen‑free hour before bed) to improve morning mood baseline.  
  – Prioritize self‑improvement/motivation content in early‑day slots to counter low morning scores.  
  – Schedule mindful breaks or guided meditation around midweek peaks in escapism and doom scrolling.  
  – Use weekly heatmaps to identify “high‑risk” days (e.g., Mondays/Wednesdays/Saturdays) and pre‑plan coping strategies (journaling, brief walks).  
  – Limit exposure to “trending” or “controversial” material when therapy/support or mindfulness content yields better emotional returns.
